# Example: Career Decision

> This shows what the full AI prompt produces (vs the quick web app output).

## Situation

Senior engineer at a large tech company. Offer from a startup (30% pay cut, significant equity). Also up for promotion at current company. 34 years old, has a mortgage.

## AI Simulation

### Option A: Join Startup

**Projected process:**
- Months 1-3: Learning curve, broader responsibility, equity is paper money
- Months 4-12: Company hits milestones → equity rises; or misses → stress builds
- Year 2-3: Exit event, down round, or stable growth stage

**Outcomes:**
- ✅ Best: $500M+ exit → your 0.5% = $2.5M
- ⚖️ Likely: Steady growth → equity worth $100-300K
- ❌ Worst: Company folds in 18 months → lost $150K salary + career reset

**Risk: HIGH** (30% pay cut with a mortgage is significant)

**Alternate self:** "The startup year was terrifying — savings dropped every month. But I learned more in 12 months than 4 years at BigCo. Even if it fails, I'm now a 'startup engineer' with options."

### Option B: Stay for Promotion

**Projected process:**
- Year 1: Promotion likely goes through
- Year 2-3: New role, more meetings, less coding
- Year 4+: Comfortable income, growing sense of "is this it?"

**Outcomes:**
- ✅ Best: +$50K TC, stable, keeps mortgage safe
- ⚖️ Likely: +$30-40K, predictable trajectory
- ❌ Worst: Golden handcuffs — too comfortable to leave by 45

**Risk: LOW** (financially safe, but high regret risk)

**Alternate self:** "The promotion came through. I bought a nicer car. But 3 years later, my startup friends are years ahead in career growth. The money is good. The regret is real."

### Decision Brief

- **Your tendency**: Stay (risk-averse with financial decisions)
- **Recommended**: Take startup if you have 12+ months savings
- **Bias alert**: Status quo bias — overweighing mortgage risk vs regret risk
- **Compromise**: Negotiate later start date to save buffer, or ask for smaller pay cut
